#+TITLE: Calendars, holiday sources and views

How the commands are put together, where each holiday comes from, and what is
deliberately left out.

* Terms

- *Grid calendar*: the calendar whose days are laid out in the grid. Jalali for the
  =jcal*= commands (Saturday first), Gregorian for the =gcal*= commands (Monday first).
  See [[file:../jalali_calendar_cli/calendars.py][calendars.py]].
- *Holiday source*: answers "which days in this Gregorian date range are days off, and
  what are they called", and says which weekdays are its weekend. Sources are keyed by
  =datetime.date=, so any source can be drawn on any grid calendar. See
  [[file:../jalali_calendar_cli/sources/][sources/]].
- *Primary / secondary source*: a view shows one source, or two. The primary is drawn
  as holidays always were (text color); the secondary is overlaid (background).
- *Month model*: everything about one month that a formatter needs, decided once
  ([[file:../jalali_calendar_cli/model.py][model.py]]): each day's Gregorian date and its
  roles (primary holiday, primary weekend, secondary holiday, secondary weekend, both
  free), the weekday labels, the title and subtitle, and the footnotes.
- *Formatter*: turns a month model into output. There are two:
  [[file:../jalali_calendar_cli/render_ansi.py][render_ansi.py]] for the terminal and
  [[file:../jalali_calendar_cli/render_html.py][render_html.py]] for the HTML page. Neither
  decides anything about holidays, which is what keeps the terminal and the page in
  agreement.

* The commands

Each command is a grid calendar plus one or two sources
([[file:../jalali_calendar_cli/commands.py][commands.py]]):

- =jcal=: Jalali grid, Iran.
- =gcal-lmu=, =gcal --sources lmu=: Gregorian grid, LMU.
- =gcal=, =gcal-all=: Gregorian grid, any one or two sources by =--sources= (default =lmu=).
- =gcal-lmu-iran=: Gregorian grid, LMU, with Iran overlaid.
- =jcal-iran-lmu=: Jalali grid, Iran, with LMU overlaid.
- the =-all= variants: the same, as a year view
  ([[file:../jalali_calendar_cli/year_view.py][year_view.py]]), which replaced
  =jcal_all.pl= and renders all twelve months in one process. The year views leave out
  the holiday list unless =--footnotes= is given, and put the other calendar's months on
  the title line, abbreviated and without years (=2026 March · Esf–Far=), so that a year
  fits a terminal. A title that would not fit the month's width keeps two lines.
- =jcal-html=: all four views as a static page
  ([[file:../jalali_calendar_cli/html_page.py][html_page.py]]).

* Iran

[[file:../jalali_calendar_cli/sources/iran.py][sources/iran.py]].

- Years in [[file:../jalali_calendar_cli/holidays.json][holidays.json]] come from it. It is
  copied from the published official calendar, and it wins over anything computed.
- Other years come from the [[https://pypi.org/project/holidays/][holidays]] package
  (=holidays.Iran=). Its Islamic dates are estimates, marked "(estimated)". Compared with
  the bundled data for 1402 to 1405 it agreed on every date except Martyrdom of Imam Ali
  in 1405 (official 12-09, computed 12-10) and the 2023 heat closure (official Aug 2-3,
  computed Aug 1-2). =--no-holidays-fallback= turns the fallback off.
- 30 Esfand, which exists only in leap years, is always added: it is an official holiday
  as part of the 29 Esfand to 4 Farvardin span.
- The weekend defaults to Thursday and Friday. Officially only Friday is the weekend,
  but Thursday is off in practice for much of the country, and a law making it official
  has been pending since 2024. =--iran-weekend fri= gives the official reading.
- The package is imported only when a year needs it, so =jcal= for a covered year does
  not pay its ~65 ms import (a test checks this).

* LMU Munich

[[file:../jalali_calendar_cli/sources/lmu.py][sources/lmu.py]]. Days off for LMU staff
employed under TV-L:

- Bavarian public holidays from the =holidays= package (=Germany(subdiv="BY")=), with its
  "catholic" category: Assumption Day (Aug 15) is a public holiday only in Bavarian
  municipalities with a predominantly Catholic population, and Munich is one. Checked
  against the Bavarian ICS feed at https://ics.tools/Feiertage/bayern.ics for 2026 to
  2028.
- December 24 and 31: TV-L § 6 Abs. 3 releases employees from work on these days with
  continued pay.

Left out on purpose:

- lecture-free days and lecture periods: they are not days off for staff;
- Shrove Tuesday (Faschingsdienstag): not a day off under TV-L, although some units
  close at noon;
- Repentance Day (Buß- und Bettag): a school holiday in Bavaria, not a public holiday.

* Overlay colors

One channel per source, so they can be read independently:

- text color is the primary source: red for a holiday, magenta for its weekend;
- background is the secondary source: a strong tint for a holiday, a faint tint for its
  weekend, which covers the whole column, from the weekday label down through the empty
  cells before the first and after the last day;
- a gold background means both sides are free that day, by holiday or by weekend. It
  replaces the secondary source's tint on that day, and the text keeps the primary
  source's color.

Gold was first a text color laid over the secondary background; gold text on light blue
was hard to read (a contrast ratio near 2:1), and a gold block is the more visible answer
to the question these views exist for: when are both sides free. The cost is that a gold
day no longer shows whether the secondary side is off for a holiday or its weekend; the
column and the footnotes still say.

Today is marked in a way that leaves both channels alone: bold and underlined in the
terminal's overlay views, an outline ring on the page. (Single-source terminal views keep
the old background highlight for today, since nothing else uses the background there.)

The colors are presets in =render_ansi.py= and CSS variables in
[[file:../jalali_calendar_cli/web/theme.css][web/theme.css]]; every terminal color can be
overridden with the =--KEY-color= and =--KEY-true-color= options.

* Adding a holiday source

1. Write a class in =sources/= with =tag= (the footnote tag), =label= (the name on the
   page), =calendar= (its own calendar, for the native dates in footnotes), =weekend=
   (Python weekdays, Monday is 0) and =events(start, end) -> {date: name}=. Cache per
   year: the year views and the page ask for each year many times.
2. Register its name in =make_source= and =SOURCE_NAMES= in
   [[file:../jalali_calendar_cli/cli.py][cli.py]]. It is then available as
   =gcal --sources NAME= or =gcal --sources NAME,OTHER=, and likewise =gcal-all=.
3. Optionally add shortcut commands in =commands.py= and =pyproject.toml=, and a view to
   =VIEWS= in =html_page.py=.

* The HTML page

=jcal-html --out DIR= pre-renders every month of every view for last year through two
years ahead. The script ([[file:../jalali_calendar_cli/web/app.js][web/app.js]]) only
chooses which months to show, marks today from the browser's clock (so a page generated
months ago is still right about today), shows each day's tooltip, and remembers the view, the theme and the
checkboxes in =localStorage=: "Holiday list" (off by default), and "Next month" and "Next
year's start" (on by default). The latter adds as
many of the next year's months as the year grid has columns at the current width, that
is one row: 4 on a wide screen, January alone on a phone. There is no automatic dark mode: the theme is a manual switch.

Only the view buttons and the navigation row are in the sticky header, each kept on one
line down to phone width; the color key and the checkboxes sit below it and scroll away, so
that on a phone the header does not cover half the screen. They are collapsed by default; the
gear button next to the theme switch opens them, and the choice is remembered.

The tooltips keep to the same division of labor. A day with holidays carries them in a
=data-tip= attribute; for the dates, each month carries =data-other=, where each month of
the other calendar starts in its grid (=[[1, 11, "Khordad", 1405], [22, 1, "Tir", 1405]]=
for June 2026), so the script finds any day's other date by adding days and never converts
between calendars itself. The tooltip is one element positioned by the script, not the
browser's =title= tooltip: it opens on hover with a mouse, on tap (tap again or elsewhere to
close), and on keyboard focus, and stays inside the viewport.

Generation is deterministic and writes only files whose content changed, so a second run
reports =unchanged= for all three files (tested). [[file:../site/holidays/refresh.sh][site/holidays/refresh.sh]]
runs it from a checkout of this repository into its own directory, where the output is
gitignored.

* Tests

=pytest= from the repository root. The golden files in [[file:../tests/golden/][tests/golden/]]
were captured before the renderer refactor (the =jcal= months, plain and colored) and
from the old Perl script (the =jcal-all= years), so they pin the output byte for byte.
The colored month goldens without =_fri= show the Thursday and Friday weekend; the
=_fri= ones are the old behavior, reproduced with =--iran-weekend fri=.
